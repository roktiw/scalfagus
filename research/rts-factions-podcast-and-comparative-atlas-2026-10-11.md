# RTS faction and race design — comparative atlas (podcast + reference)
Date: 2026-10-11
Source: design conversation of 2026-10-11 (Polish). Status: **research notes / interpretations, not approved gameplay nor independently verified balance results**.

## Why this document exists
The owner requested a **spoken, podcast-friendly, race-by-race** treatment of games in the supplied comparison table. The goal is to understand **who each civilization is, how it builds and fights, how asymmetry is implemented**, then compare with *StarCraft* (three strong asymmetrical races) and *Magic: The Gathering* (five-color philosophy). This is a design inspiration atlas, **not a claim that these games are identically balanced**.

## Baselines: StarCraft and MTG
**StarCraft**
- **Terran**: constructed/industrial civilization. Modular military, repairable mechanical units, varied combined-arms, flying buildings, fortification, logistics. In Scalfagus loosely **Manusians**, not a direct copy.
- **Zerg**: biological production ecosystem; larva-driven unit production, expansion and living infrastructure; living units, growth, regeneration/adaptation. In Scalfagus loosely **Blergians**, but do **not** import Tyranid-style automatic absorption of enemy genetics, one universal consuming entity, amorphous goo or mandatory hive mind.
- **Protoss**: few expensive specialists, shields, psionics, pylons/power-field infrastructure, high-technology. In Scalfagus loosely **Aetherians**, not literally energy-only beings.
- Quality of asymmetry comes from **different production, base operations, survival/repair, movement and combat**, not different unit skins. Balance is historical and iterative, not mathematically perfect.
- Distinct non-mirror faction matchups = n(n−1)/2: 3 races => 3, 4 => 6, 5 => 10, 6 => 15, 9 => 36. Mirror matches add n.

**MTG five-color wheel**
- White (W): structure, solidarity, institutions/order; Blue (U): knowledge, design, improvement/control; Black (B): self-determination, pragmatism, sacrifice; Red (R): freedom, emotion, impulse; Green (G): nature, growth, inherited/ecological patterns.
- A five-node wheel has two neighbors and two opposites per color. Use as a framework for **shared values, tensions and overlaps**, **not** as an automatic numerical balance formula or fixed alliance game mechanic.
- The owner's separate experimental color extensions (Yellow = activation/sustaining/energy; other Violet, Orange, Brown) are **speculative tags**, NOT official MTG colors or extra economic currencies.
- Owner's rough three-civilization mapping: Blergians Jund (B/R/G); Manusians Jeskai (W/U/R); Aetherians Bant (W/U/G). Fourth autonomous synthetic civilization might evoke Esper (W/U/B), with experimental Yellow for power, but no binding lore obligation.

## 1. Warcraft III — four factions
### Human Alliance
A coalition of humans, dwarves and elves; not literally one biological species. Combined arms: infantry, riflemen, knights, casters, siege, flying machines; defensive buildings and ordered expansion. Multiple peasants can accelerate construction. Nearest thematic comparison: Terran/Manusian **industrial mixed-arms**.

### Orcish Horde
Orcs, trolls, tauren and allies, with shamanism and heavily weighted melee units. Strong bodies, aggressive field play, durable but typically more expensive infantry; defensive Burrows shelter peasants and contribute to defense. The economic core remains closer to Human than to Undead. An aggressive cultural doctrine is **not automatically** a new type of life.

### Undead Scourge
Skeletons, ghouls, necromancers, abominations, creatures and siege engines; death and necromancy integral to warfare. Acolytes summon buildings, many structures require Blight; ghouls harvest lumber and can fight. This makes building, environment and worker/soldier roles visibly different. MTG comparison: strongly Black, with control-oriented Blue possibilities. **Lesson:** alternative construction and material substrate can justify a true faction.

### Night Elves
Magic/nature civilization: Wisps harvest lumber without felling trees; living Ancient buildings can uproot and move or fight. Concealment at night, shapeshifting Druids, archery, living forest infrastructure. MTG analogy: G/U/W, not a canonical MTG association. **Lesson:** buildings themselves can be units and a base can be partly mobile/living.

**Overall:** Four unmistakable styles, but not necessarily four unrelated biological ontologies. A new playable race is a complete playable economy and war doctrine, not necessarily a new species.

## 2. Age of Mythology: Retold — four launch cultures
### Greeks
Hoplites, cavalry, archers, heroes and mythological creatures such as Minotaurs, Cyclopes or Medusae; broad toolkit, favour through divine mechanics and temple/worship choices. Plays relatively classically, but mythic tech profoundly varies by major/minor god.

### Egyptians
Faraon/priests, monuments, sacred buildings, Anubites and sphinxes, war engines; monuments generate Favour, several buildings have different cost arrangements, pharaoh empowers production/construction. Sacred institutions act as an economic system.

### Norse
Warrior-centered civilization with giants, myth units, aggressive expeditionary tempo. Military units are part of the building workflow, and fighting is central to generating Favour. Building and army activity overlap by design.

### Atlanteans
Costly, more versatile workers, distinct economic efficiency and military development; ordinary soldiers can become heroes; mythic Automatons exemplify mechanical units **inside** a civilization, not their own race.

**Lesson:** Add god/doctrine paths **inside** a race; new unit families need not multiply founding civilizations. The 4-count is launch scope, not the up-to-date total of expansions.

## 3. Supreme Commander: Forged Alliance — four technologically advanced futures
### UEF
Conventional heavy industry: tanks, naval forces, artillery, fortifications, experimental giant units. Conceptually 'Terran industrial megawar' with strong conventional combined arms.

### Cybran Nation
Cybernetically altered human descendants, irregular engineering, stealth, walkers and experimental designs, including the Monkeylord spiderbot. Important comparison to Manusian **Strogg/cyborg** development paths: cybersynthetics do not automatically create an unrelated species.

### Aeon Illuminate
A human-derived culture shaped by alien/ideological/technological contact; elegant advanced weapons, field technologies and distinct doctrine, bridging high-tech humanity and spiritual-tech aesthetics.

### Seraphim
Advanced alien society with sophisticated fields and alien technology; in thematic conversations can suggest Protoss-adjacent advanced civilization, but not a direct equivalence.

All four work with **mass and energy**, engineers and factories. **Lesson:** high tactical variety works with common resource accounting. For Scalfagus retain Matter, Volatiles and Energy, letting factions differ in **conversion, flow, vulnerability, repair and topology**, not extra currencies.

## 4. The Battle for Middle-earth II — six fantasy factions
### Men of the West
Gondor/Rohan humans, flexible footmen, archers, cavalry, siege, heroes. Generalist.
### Elves
High-value archers, specialists, mobility/magic and micro-preservation of relatively valuable troops.
### Dwarves
Slow but tough fighters, siege and defensive force. **Tunnel network** changes logistics/force projection: distinct military movement without a separate physics engine.
### Goblins
Fast, cheap hordes and monsters including trolls, tunnel mobility, raid/surround pressure; numerous but fragile baseline troops.
### Isengard
Uruk-hai, industry and powerful siege, offensive formations; an industrialized biological army.
### Mordor
Cheap orcs and heavy monsters/siege, supernatural leaders, wide mix of attrition and elite monsters.

**Lesson:** Six flavorful armies can largely share the same economic primitives. Distinguish **civilization** from political army.

## 5. Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War — Soulstorm — nine armies
### Space Marines
Genetically enhanced human super-soldiers, armour, specialist squads, vehicles, elite versatility.
### Chaos Space Marines
Corrupted counterpart with mutations, daemons and magic; diverging civilizational doctrine from a related human lineage.
### Imperial Guard
Mass infantry, heavy armour, fortification, artillery, infrastructure: conventional militarized industry.
### Orks
Aggressive living warrior-culture, improvised construction/technology, uneven but brutal equipment, numbers.
### Eldar
Ancient psionic and technological specialists, mobility, delicate elite forces, advanced transportation/fields.
### Dark Eldar
Related species lineage to Eldar, distinct raiding, cruelty, slavery, mobile warfare and doctrine — demonstrates cultural divergence within ancestry.
### Tau Empire
High-tech ranged combat, battlesuits, drones, multi-species coalition and auxiliary arms; intelligent robots need not constitute an independent playable species.
### Necrons
Ancient consciousness embodied in mechanical forms, rebuilding/teleportation, powerful technology and an economy unlike ordinary requisition-based armies. Most directly relevant to **self-sufficient machine civilization** vs simply robotic Manusian units.
### Sisters of Battle
Devotional militarized Imperial human order, flames, zeal, morale and **Faith** gameplay. Religion can be a first-class mechanical driver without being a new biological form.

**Lesson:** 9 army packages ≠ 9 substrates or fundamental minds. Explore culture/army/technology/ontology as separate axes.

## 6. Warlords Battlecry: The Protectors of Etheria — 16-race maximalist strategy
The community-developed expansion/project uses a very broad race-and-subfaction catalog; the conversation cited a creator claim of **16 races / 50+ factions**. Verify the precise contemporary roster before treating names/count as frozen canon.
The discussion catalog included:
- Humans / Empire: different human institutional/imperial militaries; not automatically biologically separate.
- High Elves: elite magical troop doctrine.
- Wood Elves: forestry, archery, nature magic.
- Dark Elves: aggressive and shadow-influenced elven doctrine.
- Dwarves: armour, engineering, fortification.
- Dark Dwarves: more destructive/occult industrial specialization.
- Orcs: force and aggression.
- Barbarians: speed and martial aggression.
- Minotaurs: powerful, large-body melee.
- Undead: death and necromancy.
- Daemons: supernatural entities.
- Plaguelords: disease and biodestruction.
- Fey: magical creatures, unusual spell and environment interactions.
- Swarm: distributed biological numbers and coordination.
- Ssrathi: reptilian society with distinctive military traditions.
- Empire or other human-oriented faction references were part of the discussion; **do not assert this inventory is an exhaustive or officially verified list**.

This portion is a record of the conversation, not a fact-checked authoritative roster. Preserve the conceptual distinction: many variations arise from mixing biology, magic, culture, hero progression and military roles; huge scope creates considerable matchup/maintenance burden.

## 7. Age of Empires II — dozens of civilizations atop common rules
All broadly human/historical societies using food, wood, gold, stone, common building classes and ages. Variety arises through civilization bonuses, unique units, missing techs, timing and matchups.
- Mongols: mobility and cavalry archers, hunting economy.
- Britons: longbow identity/ranged control.
- Franks: knights/cavalry and supporting economy.
- Byzantines: defensive diversity and counter roster.
- Japanese: strong infantry/samurai.
- Vikings: economic power, infantry/naval uniqueness.
- Chinese: flexibility/tech selection and opening complexity.
No need for a separate chemistry or resource engine for each civilization.

## What to retain / reject for Scalfagus
**Retain:** distinctive first-minute worker and base behavior (Warcraft III); internal doctrinal branching (AoM); universal Matter/Volatiles/Energy simulation (SupCom); tunnel/supply and territorial logistics as military mechanics (BFME II); differences between biology, civilization and army (Dawn of War); high variety via shared archetype matrix (Warlords); viable bonuses and tech tradeoffs (AoE II).
**Reject:** new race only from visual skin or one exotic unit; adding every Robot/Strogg/Akira/Predator-like unit family as a race; mandatory hive mind; race-specific free resources or resurrection; claiming odd race count produces inherent balance; shipping 5 factions in MIN without a playable core.

## Podcast framing / spoken summary
Imagine a Warcraft Human worker accelerating a construction with coworkers, an Undead Acolyte summoning the construction on corrupted ground, a Night Elf building walking off to defend the base; then a Norse soldier becoming a builder, a Dwarf network transporting an army through tunnels, a Cybran engineer assembling the next impossible machine, a Necron getting rebuilt out of ancient hardware. That sequence demonstrates real asymmetry more memorably than a damage-table comparison. The deeper design question is not 'what does the army look like?' but **'how does the civilization work when not fighting?'**
