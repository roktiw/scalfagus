# Scalfagus — Fourth Civilization Candidate: Independent Machine People
Date: 2026-10-11
Status: **strong owner-preferred fourth-race concept; NOT a commitment to ship four playable factions in MVP**.
Working names: Noemids, Machine Guilds, Automata / Axioms (all temporary; original naming and trademark review later).

## 0. The owner proposal (captured as intent)
If Scalfagus adds a fourth race, prefer **a civilization of independently thinking AI robots**. They could originate from humanity's technology on Terra, become self-sustaining and eventually leave Terra for the stars. Their engineering knowledge may be so advanced that Aetherians once encountered, exchanged ideas with or learned from them, especially regarding sophisticated flying craft and starships.

The faction inherits *aspects* of all existing three, while being genuinely independent:
- From **Manusians / Terran**: engineering, analytical observation, administration, modularity, logistics, industrial discipline, technology management.
- From **Blergians / Zerg**: intrinsic regeneration/self-repair, persistent local functional autonomy; even a detached tentacle/manipulator or fragment may continue its narrowly bounded task.
- From **Aetherians / Protoss**: sophisticated energy handling, advanced energy weapons and precision high-technology.
- **Aesthetic**: 1990s Z (Bitmap Brothers) — clearly identifiable, lively, sometimes comic military robots, industrial pixel-art; Stanisław Lem's *Bajki robotów* (*Fables for Robots*) — **robot persons, societies, inventors, politics, quirks, fables, self-directed intelligence**. Do not copy characters, names, icons, screenshots or designs.

Additional owner-level constitutional intent:
- NO mandatory hive mind for any Scalfagus civilization.
- NO Akira-like, civilization-wide amorphous flesh blob.
- NO universal organism consuming/absorbing everything or automatically learning all enemy genetics.
- Independent specialized units with distinct identity and behavior are paramount.
- MTG-style cards/decks/guilds/combinations are a useful model for making unit roles distinct and building synergies. See [individual-agency-and-composable-civilizations.md](individual-agency-and-composable-civilizations.md).

## 1. Civilization vs Manusian robot unit: the critical distinction
A **Manusian robot** is a machine unit produced as a component of a human-origin industrial civilization; its economy, production authority and cultural/political context belong to the Manusian faction. It may still be independently intelligent.

The **Machine People** are a self-governing, self-reproducing (under finite resource costs), self-maintaining civilization with independent origins as a *polity*, its own institutions, guilds, design archives and methods of planning/production. Some members may descend from Manusian machines, but they do not need human officers or Manusian factories to continue existing.

A political secession is an optional historical trigger, not a separate physical creation mechanism. Do not imply that all Manusian AI must rebel or that all robots share one central mind.

### Test of faction independence
A fourth playable faction is justified only if it introduces a distinct **economic/territory decision loop** beyond merely robot-shaped units:
- ***Maintain / repair / recover / remanufacture:*** field workshops and mobile guild ateliers produce new limbs, recover material from damaged equipment and reassemble citizens from compatible parts, with explicit cost/transfer constraints.
- ***Identity continuity with risk:*** a robot's local mind persists in its body; optional backups store a *versioned individual pattern*, not one collective consciousness. Rebuilding requires available hardware, energy, time, authorized memory data, and can lose experience if backup stale.
- ***Distributed craft networks:*** autonomous units can work in isolated teams with their own operational logic; optional synchronization improves recipes, diagnosis and logistics without being necessary for agency.
- ***Embodied modularity:*** specialized replaceable assemblies are a central visible mechanic: scout eye, articulated loader, repair arm, energy projector and shield frame have distinct jobs/costs/counters.

The user should see this simply as **robots fixing, swapping and rebuilding themselves**. Advanced details available only in optional UI inspectors and simulation telemetry.

## 2. Machine civilization as a culture, not one algorithm
Every robot is potentially a distinct person, worker, explorer, inventor or soldier; individuality is central. Robot societies may contain competing maker guilds, elected councils, independent workshops, schools of engineering, libraries and explorers. Cultural traits: humor, curiosity, stubbornness, duty, craftsmanship, order, opportunism and disagreement. Conflict and learning can happen inside the civilization without instantly becoming an extra playable race.

Inspirational moodboards: Z's visual readability and cartoon personalities; Lem's witty robotic fairy tales, clever constructors, robot kings, autonomous machine civilizations. Oppose both the Borg-like mandatory total assimilation and the monolithic synthetic supermind trope.

### Social/AI architecture
- AI **per unit**: local sensor state, doctrinal rules, specialist actions, short memory/maintenance status; bounded and deterministic in core game.
- Optional **team** coordination: explicit orders, known signals, fog-of-war-limited reports, formations and guild specialisms.
- Optional **faction strategy AI**: budgets, decisions, repair priorities, exploration, production goals.
- Coordination != cognitive fusion. Communications loss degrades updates and throughput; locally capable robots retain rational behavior.
- Fully manual RTS must work and must not force a robot collective-autonomy control style on the player.
- Optional LLM controls strategic API later; it does not redefine the units as a super-consciousness.

## 3. Cross-faction inheritance without losing identity
| Inspiration | Machine interpretation | Difference from source |
|---|---|---|
| Manusian observation / logistics / modules | Sensors, field workshops, design libraries, explicit orders, route-planned cargo | Machine citizenship and remanufacture are first-class; their societal dependence on *humans* is gone |
| Blergian living repair / regeneration | Automatic diagnosis and component self-repair, temporary fragments operating locally | Mechanical not biological; parts and Energy are limited, does not grow infinite new robots |
| Aetherian energy precision and aerial craft | Capacitors, field stabilization, beam weapons, distributed power conditioning | Engineered electrical/field systems rather than intrinsically psionic resonance or religious ascension |
| MTG Esper (W/U/B) | Constitution / observation / controlled demolition-reconstruction | A political and engineering metaphor; does not grant free rebuild or one universal mind |
| Additional Red flavor (R) | Robot eccentricity, autonomy, creative inventors, slapstick tactical initiative | Not all robots need to be impulsive |
| Owner's experimental Yellow | Energy, activation, transmission, sustaining / charging | Still only three in-game resource currencies |

An equivalent Blergian organ and robot manipulator may share a concept of local autonomy while using totally different appearance, inputs, vulnerabilities and costs.

## 4. Signature play loop: independent, repairable machine society
**Proposed core loop:** **Explore -> Build/assemble -> Operate -> Diagnose -> Salvage/repair -> Refit -> Continue**.

Not a free resurrection button. Destroyed parts can be partially salvaged; carrying a replacement costs Matter; a high-energy emitter costs Energy and may need Volatiles in its manufacture; reboot/repair consumes time. Energy is stored and transported, **not a mandatory electrical grid**. Inventory and production are physically bounded.

### MVP of this fourth faction (POST primary single-Manusian MVP)
The minimum convincing vertical slice needs:
- One autonomous Scout robot with local AI.
- One modular Constructor who can repair a nearby machine using paid resources.
- One specialist detachable Manipulator or Eye whose severed part has a **bounded** independent action (e.g. return to repair, survey a tile, latch to nearest safe node).
- One Workshop + one Remanufacture Station (or combined to reduce UI), using the same 3 resource economy.
- One Capacitor/Emitter weapon with clear limits and counterplay.
- One test showing loss of synchronization does not destroy unit agency.
- One test showing repair and detachment conserve inventories, cannot clone objects and cannot bypass pathing/fog.
- Original Z/Lem-inspired but independently authored pixel-art silhouettes.
Full seven archetypes, distinct MIN/MID/MAX chassis and complete culture are **not** needed for this probe. Add only if this loop proves distinct from Manusian doctrine.

## 5. Machine unit family candidates (later roster direction; NOT eight new roles)
Same base seven RTS archetypes, **different machine families and selectable hardware**:
| Common role | Candidate machine template | Readable specialist action |
|---|---|---|
| Worker | Guild Hand | Carries cargo, replaces parts, repairs |
| Fighter | Rivet Brawler | Close-range durable grapple |
| Shooter | Arc Marksman | Direct energy shot with capacitor |
| Artillery | Coil Mortar | Slow area/structure shot, charge time |
| Tank | Bastion Walker | Shield and interposition |
| Flyer | Gyro Scout | Fast aerial reconnaissance; not automatically anti-air |
| Specialist | Salvage Sage | Recuperates compatible parts, restores damaged patterns |
These names are placeholders, not a new obligation to implement 7 roles x 3 sizes in MVP.

Proposed alternative specialists: isolated sensing eye; independent articulated arm; relay sentinel; historian/backup custodian; flying construction unit. **'Independent tentacle' is a mechanical analogy**, not literal tentacle-like amorphous body horror. Different parts should have recognizable shape and limited roles.

## 6. Distinguish all four civilizations at first glance
| Aspect | Blergians | Manusians | Aetherians | Independent Machines |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Civilization ideal | Living adaptation | Human-directed inventive industry | Cultivated mind/resonance | Independent artifact life and self-maintenance |
| Body/architecture | Distinct organisms; grown structures | Humans, cyborgs, vehicles, robots; factories | Advanced living minds, resonance works | Individualized robot citizens; mobile workshops, modular housings |
| Society | Specialized biological citizens/castes, not hive-mind | Humans + AI + machines in shared institutions | Diverse individuals, contemplatives, hunters | Robot guilds, inventors, artisans, explorers, disagreements |
| Default base geometry | Connected organic clusters | Distributed outposts | Core and resonant satellites | Mobile or dispersed refit/repair ateliers; retain a vulnerable repair/continuity economy |
| Production | Grow/mutate | Mine/build/assemble/loadout | Resonant creation/transformation | Assemble/repair/salvage/rebody |
| Damage response | Biological healing at cost | Human repair, medical care, field resupply | Shields/expensive transformative recovery | Intrinsic diagnostics, field repair, parts recovery at cost |
| Attack emphasis | Attrition/specialized biological assaults | Combined arms, flexible conventional weapons | Positional psi/field precision | Modular coordinated skirmishing and precise energy firearms |
| Key weakness | Overcommitment / organism/network loss | Disrupted transport and isolated depots | Central core / costly transformations | Spare-part scarcity, disabled service stations, energy starvation, component complexity |
| Possession feel | Specialized organic movement | Familiar shooter/vehicle ergonomics | Psi abilities or hunter precision | Robot body with repair/reconfigure and limited autonomous limbs |
| No universal mind | Yes | Yes | Yes | **Yes** |

Aesthetic distinction: machine faction should avoid a generic white/gold Protoss clone, industrial green/brown Terran clone, or smooth 'infinite nanobot goo'. Look for **chunky, purposeful, modular, expressive individual silhouettes**, recognizable articulated limbs and brightly contrasted 1990s pixel-art legibility.

## 7. Potential historical link with Aetherians — do NOT canonize yet
Owner's attractive hypothesis: **Terra-born artificial beings went into space; their advanced knowledge influenced the starship/aerial craft of an Aetherian civilization**. There are multiple coherent chronologies:
- **Engineering exchange:** Aetherians already mastered resonant levitation, but machine navigators contributed control algorithms, modular shipbuilding, and high-reliability distributed flight control; Aetherians contributed protective/field technology.
- **Ancient contact:** after robot diaspora, machine expeditions contacted earlier Aetherian societies and shared shipbuilding knowledge. Must solve timeline: how long since Terra and how old are Aetherians?
- **Rediscovered ruins:** Aetherians reverse-engineered machine craft from abandoned probes/archives, while their psi disciplines evolved independently. Avoid assuming either faction owns all technology.

If the machines have more engineering **knowledge** in some domains, it does not mean their troops are always stronger. Other civilizations can have advantages in biology, perception, psyonics or military logistics. Avoid canonical one-way tech theft until lore timeline is chosen.

### Suggested first-story premise (proposal)
Machine guilds born from Terra's factories began as different independent specialist workers. Their long-duration missions required resilience and autonomy without central communications. After many generations of remanufacture, they created their own civilization and spacefaring craft. Distant machine artisans eventually met the Aetherian worlds. Both recognized mutually alien strengths: Aetherian resonance and machine engineering. Their technology exchange later influenced sophisticated aerial/starship designs without making either civilization subordinate to the other.

No forced robot rebellion, genocide or universe-wide enslavement storyline required. Different human and machine guilds may maintain friendship, rivalry or estrangement.

## 8. Mechanical proofs, balance questions and anti-goals
Questions:
1. What is the irreducible gameplay difference between a Manusian robotic squad and a faction of machine citizens beyond cosmetics/bonuses?
2. Does the machine repair/refit/reclaim system add strategic choice rather than tedious micromanagement? Can player delegate it to AI?
3. What constitutes individual identity after destruction: exact memory, partial memory, copied blueprint, or production template? Can duplicates coexist?
4. What does a detached component actually know, sense and do when radio silent? Local sensors must obey fog of war.
5. How do machine 'personalities' influence tactics without unfair opaque RNG or excessive per-unit stats?
6. Can a machine be captured, hacked or persuaded? How would cyber defenses avoid single-click hard counters?
7. Can Manusians still produce robots, cyborgs, Fleshborn/Akira-inspired units and Strogg-like cyberforms without erasing faction identity?
8. Do Aetherians have natural psionics separate from machine energy systems? Why were they technologically inspired rather than simply subordinated?
9. Do robot guilds have a mobile-base advantage sufficiently separate from independent Manusian outposts?
10. Does their autonomous reconstruction outclass Blergian biological regeneration or Aetherian reversible transformations? What are clear opportunity costs?
11. How are detach/recombine operations represented in isometric pixel art with enough silhouette clarity and few animations?
12. What does the player see in a 30-second gameplay clip that proves this is a real fourth civilization?

Anti-goals:
- No central hive mind / automatic omniscience.
- No one 'AI god' possessing all robots or consolidating them into one blob.
- No free micro-unit proliferation from every damaged joint, endless autonomous replication or free resurrection.
- No fourth mandatory resource named 'data' or 'scrap'; data may be tech prerequisite, while scrap is recoverable Matter.
- No 105-unit/five-race delivery commitment. Fourth machine civilization is a **candidate** after three-faction MIN Alpha.
- No direct copies of Z or Lem's character designs, named characters or signature settings.

## 9. Recommendation and milestones
**Keep first MVP** one Manusian faction with seven MIN-chassis archetypes *as eventual MVP target* (smaller playable proof first) and limited possession. The fourth faction adds **zero mandatory first-MVP features** beyond shared component contracts when warranted.
- Next: concept art / silhouettes of 3–4 original machine specialists and a 30-second four-way identity pitch.
- Then: headless test of detached-manipulator and repair-to-recover cycle; compare with same functionality achievable by Manusian engineer + robot.
- Then: two-bot/four-bot 10–15 minute skirmish against Manusian prototype; players explain differences without lore.
- Gate: promote as fourth playable civilization only if economy and territory control are truly distinct, and the loop survives performance, balance and UX tests.

**No unapproved change to existing three-race baseline or canonical `design/factions.yaml`.** This is a candidate fourth race that the owner prefers *if* four are ever implemented.
