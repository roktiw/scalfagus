## Subsequent raw-ingress correction: DEC-039

The 2026-10-12 owner decision [DEC-039](org-plug-holes-and-eth-three-ingress-methods-2026-10-12.md) keeps the **one ETH Ether Network** and all conserved local inventories and transfer rules here, but **supersedes the historical Nexus-only raw ingress wording**. Future ETH may enter raw cargo into elevated Ether via (1) physical Nexus delivery at zero incremental upload Energy cost, (2) an eligible drone paying 33% of its own shield to inject a bounded package at an Obelisk tip, or (3) expensive powered automatic Obelisk pick-up of eligible ground cargo. Earlier Nexus-only examples here are historical baseline fixtures, not prohibitions against DEC-039. The ORG vessel network remains bidirectional (DEC-038); Plug Hole interfaces for eligible MIN/MID/MAX workers are the current **assistant-preferred** port design, with the user's final mouth-versus-plug choice still open. ## 2. ETH — ONE unified physically routed Ether Network

**New owner clarification:** ETH has **one network transmitting resources, information AND Energy**. Its pylons are like Settlers relay flags: they determine valid resource travel endpoints and relay hops rather than forming three independent graphs. Pylon/Obelisk remains one ETH MID building, MAX remains a Nexus converter and local pattern core, and MIN remains physical extractor plus network-mode builder.

1. MIN gathers real solids or slow manual liquids; gas requires installed collector. It physically reaches an eligible **MAX Nexus** intake.
2. Nexus converts actual received and debited cargo into **typed Ether representation** that preserves resource ID, amount, origin and original physical form. No fourth currency and no resource type conversion by mere network transit.
3. All pylon relays along the valid route use **ONE graph**. Each edge has finite carrying bandwidth and latency; at the simulation level distinguish a packet channel kind: `resource`, `energy`, `information`. They share the same edge existence/topology and configured aggregate transmission budget (different per-kind priority/weights are balancing choices).
4. Each pylon is an **anchored relay and Farm** (adds synchronized units/buildings limit) and can be **STORAGE** (larger accessible local Ether holding or throughput) or **TURRET** (defense). Changing mode requires paid meditation and remains a consequential choice. The safest newest recommended default: **the basic relay and information connection stays active in both modes**, but turret has reduced resource transfer and cannot act as an open local cargo depot; this avoids turning its defensive use into a free network-cut trap. Exact `turret_relay_rate_multiplier` is TBD.
5. Connected graph stocks **gradually equalize by actual usable capacities and edge rates** (existing recommendation), while explicit construction orders reserve locally reachable actual typed inputs before background transfers. No instant universal bank. On sever, compute components, no magical redistributions: each retains its own physical node inventory and permitted edge escrow.
6. An initialized expansion with Nexus + pylons and cached plans can continue using **only resources in its newly isolated component**. Unknown blueprints require an actual path to a known library; connections are through the *same* Ether Network.
7. A newly created construction site must have an eligible ETH MIN holding the blue beam to an authorized large node (latest 1–2–3 assistants by construction thirds). Typed Ether is really reserved and routed to the construction receiver. **If its last mandatory link breaks, the unfinished entity is destroyed as a construction object; its entire material ingredient allocation ejects *at the actual site* as physical ETH orbs.** Already spent operational Energy and time are not regenerated. No duplicate Nexus credit.
8. If a resource packet is presently inside **link-edge escrow** when a live Ether edge physically breaks, it may materialize as a visible orb at the rupture point, with explicit finite loss. Other packets safely stored in surviving pylons **remain there**. The link event must not simultaneously cancel back to sender AND eject the same packet.
9. Mobility: Pylon/Obelisk **cannot move in MVP**. Later, a paid technology might enable relocation **only along an existing connected Ether link through meditation**, with node support, inventory and downtime rules; not universal building teleportation.

**Meaning of "uniform distribution":** equal filling fraction by eligible node capacity and source resource type (proposed), *not* equality of raw amounts where a Nexus holds much more than a pylon. A cut before synchronization ends isolates the actual not target balance.

**Important supersession:** The older idea of two independent ETH control and freight graph components is **not owner-approved** after this clarification. Use one connected topology, with packet classes/service quality; storage-versus-turret may restrict handling throughput but should not silently generate a second geographical topology under the standard proposed mode. The earlier per-hop sender reservation simplification was a technical option only; drop-on-damaged-edge requires separately accounted **in-flight escrow**.

### ETH example: split a 2/4 network

Six identical pylon inventories each hold 20 units of one typed resource, 120 total. Breaking the connector between pylons 2 and 3 creates component inventories `40` and `80` (minus only any actual in-flight packet loss, if present). Reconnect: route transfers gradually to their target filling percentages without creation.

### ETH example: worker cancels at 66%

Suppose `15 solids` and `6 liquids` have **actually arrived in committed on-site construction custody**, and `5 E` already spent as channeling work. The cancellation yields `15 + 6` locally collectible physical ETH orbs of corresponding form, progresses to 0%, **never refunds 5 already-burnt E**. If an additional `3 solids` are merely *reserved in a remote pylon*, not yet in site custody, they **stay reserved/released at that real remote holder** rather than materializing a second time at the build. The UI must accurately distinguish planned cost vs actually site-committed material. No invisible remote Ether withdrawal to create orphan material instantly.

## 3. ORG — two linked biological responsibilities and TWO mitochondria

**New owner clarification:** unlike the old "vessels carry mainly nutrition" recommendation, **live ORG veins carry resources AND information AND energetic food** — the same broad classes of useful transfers as ETH, but **as physically moving matter through living tissue**, with capacity, pressure/throughput, vulnerability and delay. ORG retains its long-range worker/mother supply loops; vessels are **not** a transmutation magic bank.

### Subsystem A — live vessel network

- A building/cocoon may start only where a live connected vessel physically reaches a valid growth cell; original home Nexus roots remain. The graph consists of real growing destructible segments and finite biological carriers/fluids.
- Individual typed parcels are pushed through real vein edges at actual finite transit rate, occupy actual live-vessel capacity and must be accepted into physical destination storage. Structural, signaling and nutrient flow use this same living network substrate.
- **Unlike ETH**: no mandatory cargo conversion at Nexus to a shareable Ether representation; no abstract all-node global balance; physical nourishing home input is still needed for remote expansion in addition to continuous rooted connection. ORG cannot directly "teleport" solid crates between any two veins; transport packet undergoes per-edge finite travel and packaging/size constraints, with workers and flying MAX mothers as an important long-haul bulk path.
- When vein breaks, parcels **in the cut segment** may spill as ORG tetrahedral drops, and distal tissues enter a finite local-reserve state; only actually withering buildings gradually emit some embedded materials as drops. Not every building immediately explodes on mere temporary severance.

### Subsystem B — mitochondrial Energy production

Owner explicitly requests "**build 2 mitochondria to have energy**". **Proposed implementation interpretation for playtests:** each *metabolically active power region* needs **two completed, connected mitochondrion organ structures** before full network Energy production for sustained operation unlocks. They generate Energy/metabolic power and feed it through living vessels, at actual finite recipe/rate and upkeep.

Critical **bootstrap safety:** a new game must allow enough home Nexus latent energy / seed organ supplies to **construct the first two mitochondria** without requiring fully functional mitochondria beforehand. Whether **exactly two per colony, at least two per connected root network, or a two-module pair within one macrobuilding** is *not explicitly fixed* by the user's phrase; initial candidate is **at least two per locally powered vessel component** with starting reserves and explicit warnings. Don't turn a remote vessel cut into an unavoidable instant wipe if one mitochondrion survives; reduced output / buffered local Energy is an alternate penalty to benchmark.

Supply difference: **Metabolic support capacity** still comes from completed connected living buildings, while the mitochondrial pair produces actual Energy/biological nourishment. *Building a third mitochondrion can increase output/redundancy without creating extra primary resource categories.* Nutrition from home is still a real timed delivery obligation, not automatically satisfied by having a local mitochondrion.

### ORG interruption and salvage

- Cocoon caretakers 3→2→1, must tend an existing vessel-grown spore; last caretaker leaves/dies/scares off → development pauses then reverses over time. The bud enters `WITHERING`, periodically **ejecting some portion of already embodied resources** and losing others to metabolic death; the emitted tetrahedra are now free map loot.
- A vital artery cut isolates buildings; each dying bud/organ **spits some recoverable stock over successive decay ticks** until saved or destroyed. A repaired vein can halt this where there are sufficient live reserves; no guaranteed global full salvage.
- Organic losses should feel like a decomposing organism, not a building being dismantled with screws. Visual timelapse: branch fades, bursts/oozes tetrahedra one at a time, scavengers race in.

## 4. MAN — THREE separable logistical responsibilities, but not three mandatory micromanagement minigames

Owner proposes "**Universal Man Unit (UMU)**": the basic land-mobile module is a power plant and either warehouse or bunker, and the race maintains separate (1) producer/UMU, (2) real material supply, (3) electrical plus information optical lines.

### System 1: UMU — Universal Man Unit

- Basic **UMU has wheels/tracks, ground-only mobility**; deployed it can be an Energy generator with **WAREHOUSE** or **BUNKER** functional posture, using actual transformations with occupied setup time, opportunity cost and footprint.
- This is intended to *reduce* the asset catalog: **prototype UMU as a generator-module variant of the existing MAN MAX mobile warehouse/bunker economic chassis**, not a new large `Power Dock` AND old MAX. This preserves MAX large physical cargo and multi-soldier bunker identities but supplies a costed finite generation kit; exact starting availability/mass/energy output TBD.
- Power in BUNKER mode may be reduced and local warehouse service locked; no full generation + full bulk storage + full heavy garrison + mobile motion at once. While moving, generation and anchored grid coupling pause. All forms keep finite real inventories; bunker conversion cannot hide cargo inside nonexistent space. Heavy MAX still has limited movement speed, cost and only ground locomotion.
- MAN MID retains its *separate* high-throughput 3/5-tile belt **OR** one-soldier bunker **OR** outward sentry wall. MIN is the excavator/builder/repairer, not a portable free generator.

### System 2: ordinary physical construction resources

- Actual source → MIN/transport carts or MID conveyor → UMU in open warehouse mode, other actual Nexus component storage, MAX or construction-side material lots.
- To assemble a building, MIN has to reach/pick up real materials in its own bag, from the nearest **eligible accessible** UMU/depot/transporter, or physically fetch directly from source or installed gas rig. A powered fiber link **does not** pay for a building out of a remote infinite account.
- MAN interrupted construction preserves exact % and physically installed material. Loose pre-delivered crates remain visibly arranged around site: **any race can steal**. Installed frame requires actual `SALVAGE/EXCAVATE` repeated worker action after authorized abandonment/damage/destruction, which yields a reduced quantity of recoverable cubes (or repack after foreign salvage).

### System 3: power + data lines, distinct from cargo

**Technology distinction**: a glass fiber can carry *data*, but heavy electrical power normally needs a conductor, not mere optical fiber. Preserve the user's desired **"fiber optic for Energy and information" as a single hybrid line** composed of optical signal fibers plus conductive power strands in the same visible cable trunk / umbilical. A single `CONNECT` order establishes both over one routed physical cable, yet **Energy/power and information remain separately budgeted services**. Cargo does not travel through it.

**Recommendation for low micro:** deployed UMUs auto-snap cable terminals to nearby modules; longer-distance work requires **one** manual cable-run planning order (SCVs lay/repair it in stages), not separate data wire and power wire placement. Cables have actual map routes/collisions, damage, bandwidth and repair. Remote outpost can be autonomous if its own UMU and local materials exist — no compulsory mother cable. During cable loss, locally charged batteries maintain bounded operation; remote information and coordination diminish separately from Energy.

**MAN tradeoff:** easiest physically moving base, hardest to design efficiently. A player who optimizes all 3 systems gains powerful throughput, but early game still offers `simple construction auto-fetch` and auto-connected short tethers. The difference between optimal macro and minimal viable play **must not become a mandatory grind**.

## 5. Salvage and match-long economy across all three networks

**Global model:** all three factions can loot any faction's ejected containers; shape shows origin, color identifies source physical form. Each salvage/destruction event moves *actual, typed finite inventory* through `embedded / carried / Ether stored / vein moving / cable-independent cargo / edge escrow / dropped stack / lost` states.

### Unit/building loss pathways

- Destroy ORG unit, mother, vessel or dying bud: drop surviving physical stored cargo and some progressively reclaimed constituent input as tetrahedra.
- Destroy MAN unit, UMU, belt or unfinished construction: drop loose stocked cubes immediately, leave physical ruins that can be excavated to yield fractions of embedded materials.
- Destroy ETH pylon/Nexus, interrupt beam-built site or Ether edge with in-flight inventory: affected resource quantity condenses as spherical orbs, in some events with lossy destruction. An **ETH interrupted unfinished site specifically ejects 100% of already site-committed material inputs immediately**. Its channel time/consumed operating Energy are not restored.
- Claiming owner-neutral drops still requires pickup time, compatible handling and actual trip back to storage/Nexus; a colored orb isn't free Ether in the grid until reintroduced at a Nexus.

### Limited conservation and positive loop bans

Let `R` be actual tracked resource stock. `WORLD_SOURCE + INVENTORIES + EMBEDDED + LIVE_NETWORK + EDGE_ESCROW + DROPS + IRRECOVERABLE_CONSUMED_LOST` cannot increase after a purely internal transfer or destroy→salvage step. Any generated resources must result from explicit author-approved external sources or recipes.

**Sample salvage yields are NOT approved:** loose intact cargo 100% still physically exists; ETH incomplete site 100% committed material ejects (spent operational Energy excluded); ORG dying biomass 20–50%; MAN wreck excavation 35–55%; fully destroyed completed assets 20–45%; safe dismantle 50–70%.

**Risk:** debris recovery adds strategy but could prolong matches, causing infinite exploit loops, snowballing and loot spam. Each closed rebuild/destroy/recycle cycle must return strictly less than its inputs (except cancellation of an *uncompleted* ETH job, where its materials were never made into a finished asset). Mandatory recovery cost and contested world locations protect the strategic clock. Use domination/defeat conditions and visual coalescing stacks.

## 6. Test priority

A. **ETH single network** transmits typed Ether, knowledge and Energy across the same physical relay route. Six equal pylons holding 20 and cut into 2/4 conserve 40/80 before any edge-escrow fallout. Construct cancel at 66% ejects committed matter at site, not remote auto-refund.

B. **ORG vessel cargo** flows finite hop-by-hop and breaks leak physical cargo; disconnected colonies require vessels AND real home-origin nutrition. Build two mitochondria with starter reserves before requiring mitochondrial energy and ensure no self-softlock.

C. **MAN UMU** is the single existing heavy ground depot/bunker family augmented with a finite Energy generator module, never a free extra universal unit. Data/power share one visible hybrid cable trunk but retain separate service accounting, and physical materials never traverse it. Destroy a prepared site: loose crates stealable, frame excavated by any faction.

D. **Loot macro balance**: compare 10%, 30%, 50%, 80% recovery ratios, build times, raiding risk and average 1v1 duration; disabled loot, contestable loot and auto-collect variants. Ensure one-sided cleanup does not make the winning army untouchably wealthy.
